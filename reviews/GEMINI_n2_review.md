### ATTACK VECTOR 1: The "Integral-Closure / $t$ is polynomial" step
**Status: Sound.**
*Attack:* Could $t$ fail to be globally regular if $F$ has wild fibers or non-properness (as Keller maps often do)?
*Resolution:* The geometric behavior at infinity is irrelevant here because the step is executed entirely algebraically. $t^2 - h \circ F = 0$ is a monic polynomial equation with coefficients in $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$. Since $\mathbb{C}[x]$ is a UFD, it is unconditionally integrally closed in its fraction field $L$. Since $t \in L$ and is integral over $\mathbb{C}[x]$, it must be an element of $\mathbb{C}[x]$. This is a mathematically ironclad bypass of the geometric non-properness problem. 

### ATTACK VECTOR 2: Could $h \circ F$ be a unit (making $Z(t)$ empty)?
**Status: Sound.**
*Attack:* If $Z(t) = \emptyset$, Step 3 fails because there is no component $\Gamma$ to trace.
*Resolution:* In $\mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_n]$, the only units are the non-zero constants $\mathbb{C}^*$. In Step 1, the author establishes that $h$ cannot be constant (otherwise $[K':K]=1$). Therefore, $t$ is a non-constant polynomial. Over an algebraically closed field, Krull's Hauptidealsatz (or the Weak Nullstellensatz) guarantees that every non-constant polynomial has a non-empty zero locus of pure codimension 1. Therefore, $Z(t)$ is definitively non-empty.

### ATTACK VECTOR 3: Could $Z(t)$ map INTO the singular locus of the discriminant?
**Status: Sound.**
*Attack:* If $\Delta = \overline{F(\Gamma)}$ lies in the singular locus of $Z(h)$, then the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^n, \xi_\Delta}$ is not a Discrete Valuation Ring (DVR), $h$ is not a uniformizer, and $v_\Delta(h) \neq 1$, completely destroying the parity contradiction in Step 4.
*Resolution:* This is dimensionally impossible. Because $h \in \mathbb{C}[p]$ is explicitly constructed as *squarefree*, the algebraic set $Z(h)$ is a reduced hypersurface in $\mathbb{C}^n$ (pure dimension $n-1$). The singular locus of a reduced hypersurface has codimension at least 1 inside the hypersurface (i.e., dimension $\le n-2$). 
Because $F$ is a Keller map, it has discrete (hence finite) fibers. Finite dominant morphisms preserve dimension, so $\dim F(\Gamma) = \dim \Gamma = n-1$. Therefore, $\Delta$ is an irreducible set of dimension $n-1$, which cannot possibly be swallowed by the $\le n-2$ dimensional singular locus. The generic point $\xi_\Delta$ is definitively a smooth point of $Z(h)$. 

### ATTACK VECTOR 4: Is $v_\Gamma(h \circ F) = 1$ actually forced by etaleness?
**Status: Sound.**
*Attack:* Even if $\xi_\Delta$ is smooth, does the unramified pullback strictly guarantee valuation 1? 
*Resolution:* Yes. Because $\det \operatorname{Jac} F \neq 0$ everywhere, $F$ is everywhere unramified. By the definition of an unramified morphism, the induced map on local rings sends the maximal ideal of the target to generate the maximal ideal of the source: $m_\Delta \mathcal{O}_\Gamma = m_\Gamma$. Since both rings are DVRs (established above), $h$ is a local parameter for $m_\Delta$, meaning its pullback $h \circ F$ is a local parameter for $m_\Gamma$ (up to a local unit). Thus, its valuation in $\mathcal{O}_\Gamma$ is exactly 1.

### ATTACK VECTOR 6: Group Theory in Corollary C2
**Status: Sound (and quite brilliant).**
*Attack:* Does $D_4$ really admit an index-2 subgroup containing a point stabilizer? 
*Resolution:* Let $D_4$ act transitively on 4 points. The point stabilizer $H$ has order 2 (it is generated by a reflection). $D_4$ contains three subgroups of index 2 (one $C_4$ and two $V_4$'s). By the Galois correspondence, the inclusions $H \subset V_4 \subset D_4$ perfectly correspond to subfields $K \subset K' \subset L$ where $[K':K] = [D_4 : V_4] = 2$. Theorem C forbids this intermediate field, successfully assassinating $D_4$ as a Galois closure.
