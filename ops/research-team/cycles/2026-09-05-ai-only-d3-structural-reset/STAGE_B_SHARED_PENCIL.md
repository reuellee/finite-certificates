## Why this question

The source theorem supplies a finite model but not vanishing. The shared-matrix
generic examples isolate a parent-specific gap. Actual derived normals indexed
by triples containing label e annihilate the same nonzero parent column y_e.
That incidence geometry supports a proper pencil escape when selected witness
supports use at most two distinct triples containing e.

For triple Hc0, it is enough to produce one proper escape from each point.
The choice of the escape need not be continuous over the entire parent space.
Global coherent choices would be additional requirements for a pair cohomology
contraction; do not transfer them automatically to the triple component question.

## Candidate universal lemma, not an accepted result

For every realizable uniform rank-four parent M on eight labels, every
normalized realization Y, and every proper pairwise-incomparable signature
triple S={sigma0,sigma1,sigma2} bad at Y, there are a label e and at most
two triples T containing e such that all three signatures have a nonzero
nonnegative Gordan dependence using only rows in

```
R(e,T) = {I : e not in I} union T.
```

If true, choose three normalized witnesses on those restricted rows. The
union meets label e in at most two support planes. In a frame avoiding e,
move y_e in their intersection to the endpoint of its open parent residence
interval. Supporting normals remain fixed up to positive scale; inverse
weight rescaling keeps every signature bad. A finite endpoint is a parent
bracket wall, and an unbounded affine endpoint lies on genuine projective
parent infinity. This should give triple Hc0=0 at full scope. The constructor
must prove these assertions, including rank/support degenerations and gauge.
It gives no pair-Hc1 result.

There are 21 triples through each label. Testing the 210 unordered pairs is
enough: a successful smaller T can be padded to a pair, because retaining
more normal rows cannot destroy a pre-existing nonnegative dependence.
Thus the pointwise candidate has exactly 8*210=1,680 cases. This is a finite
denominator for this local predicate, not for the global D3 problem.
