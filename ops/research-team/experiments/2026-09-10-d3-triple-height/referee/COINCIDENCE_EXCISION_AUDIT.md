# Independent deductive acceptance: all concurrence collisions

Fix ordinary occurrences of three primitive factors and their continuous
unique projective concurrence maps p,q,r on the triple zero set Z. Let
Cpq={p=q}, Cpr={p=r}, Cqr={q=r}. All three are closed in Z.

For Cpq, use the ordinary p-anchor chart. The condition that all four
Q planes also contain p is a condition only on projected parents. It cuts
out a closed subset of the open four-dimensional projected-parent base.
It imposes no height equality. The full strict parent-height fiber is still
open convex of dimension four.

First take the closed triple-coincidence subset p=q=r. The R incidences
are also projected-parent conditions, so its nonempty fibers over the
projected parent base are full four-dimensional convex height residences.
Their compactly supported degree zero vanishes; proper-support base change
therefore gives R0 f_! Q=0 and Hc0=0 for this subset.

On the complementary open part of Cpq, retain the nonzero projection of r
and its height w. In each line-bundle trivialization, the four R incidence
equations are affine in the four parent heights and w. They impose at most
four independent equations on five variables. Every nonempty fiber is an
open convex set of dimension at least one, so again R0 f_! Q=0 and Hc0=0.
This computation uses the ambient closed projected-parent conditions times
RP2 as target; it does not assume that an arbitrary image is locally compact.
Line-bundle transition functions preserve the fiber calculation.

Localization between the closed triple-coincidence subset and this open
complement gives Hc0(Cpq;Q)=0. Re-anchoring proves the same statement for
Cpr and Cqr, because every global factor kind has a valid ordinary anchor.
The argument includes all lower affine coefficient ranks and all parent
components; no genericity or transversality is required.

Set C=Cpq union Cpr union Cqr. The degree-zero closed-cover
Mayer-Vietoris map injects Hc0(C;Q) into the direct sum of the three zero
groups, so Hc0(C;Q)=0. Equivalently, a hypothetical compact component of C
would contain a component of one of its closed covering pieces; that
component is closed in C and noncompact, a contradiction.

Thus the compact-support localization sequence gives

    Hc0(Z;Q) = Hc0(Z minus C;Q).

This removes all three coincidence loci, including q=r distinct from p.
It does not remove distinct collinear points. Collinearity alone has not
been shown to force a positive-dimensional fixed-direction height fiber.
No universal triple theorem or new numerical orbit count follows from this
excision alone.
