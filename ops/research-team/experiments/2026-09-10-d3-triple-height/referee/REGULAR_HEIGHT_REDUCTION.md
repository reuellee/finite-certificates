# Independent deductive review: regular heights and the critical locus

Let a fixed ordinary anchor identify its wall, on one original parent
normalization sector, with an open residence D subset A x R4, where A is
open in R4. Let F and G be the other two residual equations after
multiplication by nowhere-zero parent units, and let Z be their common zero
set in D.

Define K to be the closed relative subset of Z on which the two vertical
gradients d_h F,d_h G are dependent. Then Z minus K is a smooth
six-dimensional manifold and projection to A is a submersion. Each of its
connected semialgebraic components is open in this manifold. Its image in A
is nonempty and open, hence is not compact. Thus no component of Z minus K
is compact, and H_c^0(Z minus K; Q)=0.

If additionally H_c^0(K; Q)=0, the localization exact sequence for the
closed/open decomposition gives H_c^0(Z; Q)=0. The same reasoning works
over the finitely many clopen sign sectors. Since the actual ordinary-anchor
model has a global open affine four-dimensional base on each sector, no
assumption that a compact projective base has noncompact open components is
being substituted here.

Changing F or G by a nowhere-zero smooth factor does not change the rank
test on Z: its vertical derivative there is multiplied by that factor. Thus
the locus K is independent of the unit representatives used for the two
residual factors. Ordinary-occurrence replacements therefore cause no
ambiguity in this reduction.

Verdict: the reduction is valid for all original factor kinds by the anchor
extension in ANCHOR_COVERAGE_AUDIT.md. The additional critical-locus vanishing
is unproved. It does not follow from the regular-locus result, from generic
rank, or from a dimension count. Compact components can consist of a
noncompact regular open piece together with a compact critical boundary.
No original diagonal or new triple orbit is credited by this reduction.
