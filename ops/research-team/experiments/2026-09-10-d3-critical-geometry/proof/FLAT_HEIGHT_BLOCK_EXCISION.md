# Excision of a whole-height flat block

## Theorem

Fix any three distinct primitive factors P,Q,R and ordinary occurrences for
them on an original normalized uniform parent component X. Use the accepted
P-contraction chart, with four projected coordinates A and four free parent
heights h in a full open convex residence C_A. Let F_Q(A,h) be the raw ordinary
Q determinant. Define

    E_Q={A: F_Q(A,h) is identically zero as a polynomial in h},
    V_Q={Y in X intersect {P=Q=R=0}: A(Y) is in E_Q}.

Then V_Q is closed in the triple zero set and H_c^0(V_Q;Q)=0. After any of the
already-proved closed collision pieces have been removed, its remaining
relative-closed piece still has H_c^0=0 and can be excised. The same holds with
Q and R interchanged and for a finite union of these closed pieces.

This theorem assumes whole-height polynomial vanishing. A zero vertical
derivative at one parent is not asserted to imply that hypothesis. In
particular, a characteristic-kernel direction for kind48 may be stationary
but non-flat. Vertically coincident non-flat Q and R sections are also not
covered by this theorem.

## Closedness and source equivalence

The raw ordinary determinant is a polynomial of degree at most3 in the
heights, with continuous projected coefficients on every accepted
normalization sector. Indeed each normal has three height-linear components
and one height-independent component, so its four-row determinant has that
degree bound. Vanishing of its finite coefficient vector is closed in A.
The full parent-height residence is nonempty and open. Thus vanishing on that
entire residence is equivalent to polynomial identity; no invisible component
of the height fiber is omitted by the coefficient definition.

Ordinary occurrence determinants differ from their primitive factors by
nowhere-zero parent units on the uniform locus. Consequently E_Q imposes
exactly that Q holds throughout the full P-height fiber. The third condition
R is the sole remaining wall condition there. The finite clopen normalization
sectors retain all original parent components.

## Degree-zero vanishing

Let r be the unique ordinary R concurrence. On the closed piece r=p, the R
incidences depend only on projected parents and leave all four parent heights
free. Their nonempty fibers are the full open convex four-cells C_A, whose
H_c^0 vanishes.

On r different from p, retain its projected direction z in RP2 and one
private height w. The four R incidence equations are affine in (h,w), five
unknowns. For every fixed (A,z) in the closed projected condition E_Q, a
nonempty fiber is a full open convex subset of an affine space of dimension
at least1. Hence its compactly supported degree-zero cohomology is zero at
every coefficient rank. The degree-zero shriek direct image is therefore
zero, and composition of shriek pushforwards gives H_c^0=0 for this open
piece. Localization with the closed r=p piece gives H_c^0(V_Q)=0.

An open subset of a space with H_c^0=0 also has H_c^0=0, by the injection from
the closed/open localization sequence. Thus removing concurrence collisions
first does not invalidate the conclusion. Relative closedness is preserved
because V_Q was closed in the full triple zero set. Finite closed unions
again have H_c^0 injecting into the sum of their closed-piece groups.

## The global36 localization-axis subcase

The accepted global36 source representation has a core of three parent
triple planes, all passing through one central parent a. On its factor wall
their normal rank is exactly2 and their common kernel is a projective line.
Suppose the ordinary P concurrence p lies on that line. This incidence is a
closed condition on the triple zero set. In P-contraction coordinates it
states that the three core projected triple determinants vanish, a condition
only on A.

For every height lift at that A, each of the three core planes then contains
both p and the central parent. They are distinct points because p is never
an original parent. Their normals therefore lie in the two-dimensional
annihilator of this line, so the core dependence and the primitive Q wall
hold for the entire height residence. This closed localization-axis piece is
fiber-saturated and lies in E_Q. The same single-remaining-wall proof just
given proves H_c^0 directly for that piece as well.

The saturation clause matters: an arbitrary closed subset of V_Q need not
inherit vanishing merely by inclusion. Here the axis condition itself is
projected-only, so the proof applies to its full fibers.

## The global38 two-plane-axis subcase

For ordinary38=123/124/345/678, the first two planes intersect in the root
line12. Its unique concurrence is the intersection of that root line with
the axis line345 intersect678. Suppose the ordinary P concurrence p lies
on this axis. The same statement holds for every relabeling and the equivalent
ordinary occurrence.

In a full P-height fiber, planes345 and678 are fixed: each contains p and
its projected span is fixed. Their axis is consequently fixed and contains
p. Write that axis as span(p,v). The condition that the moving root line12
meet this axis is

    det(Y_1,Y_2,p,v)=0.

Moving either root parent by any multiple of p does not change this
determinant. The other six parent-height changes also leave the axis fixed.
Thus if this wall condition holds at one height, it holds at every height
in the full P residence. The condition on the projected parents is closed:
the two core planes contain p, and the root-line incidence with their fixed
axis is independent of heights. This is another fiber-saturated closed
whole-height flat block, with H_c^0=0 by the theorem.

The stated incidence uses only the ordinary circuit and the two fixed core
planes. It does not extend flatness to the different kind48 characteristic
axis, nor to arbitrary first-order gradient dependencies.
