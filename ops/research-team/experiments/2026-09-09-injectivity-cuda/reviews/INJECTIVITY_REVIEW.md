### Least-norm strict separator: accepted auxiliary lemma

For a continuous semialgebraic row matrix A(Y), strict feasibility is equivalent to nonemptiness of Q(Y)={p:A(Y)p>=1}, by positive scaling of a strict separator. A nonempty closed polyhedron has a unique squared-norm minimizer: coercivity gives existence and strict convexity gives uniqueness. Quantifier elimination applied to feasibility and the universal optimality inequality gives a semialgebraic graph.

Continuity is correctly proved using two independent comparisons. A fixed strict-margin separator at Y remains feasible nearby and gives a uniform local bound for minimizers. Every convergent subsequence of those minimizers therefore has a feasible limit at Y. For each epsilon>0, (1+epsilon)p*(Y) is itself strict-margin feasible nearby, giving the stated limsup bound. Every cluster point is consequently the unique minimizer, which proves continuity.

If (Y_n,p*(Y_n)) has a finite limit with Y_n tending to Y_0 in X, continuity of the inequalities makes that limit a strict separator at Y_0. Hence Y_0 is good and the preceding continuity argument identifies the graph limit. This proves closedness in X times R4. If Y_0 is bad, bounded minimizer norms on any subsequence would contradict that same inequality argument; therefore the norm tends to infinity.

The projection of the graph to F is a homeomorphism and is proper as a map with target F. It is generally not proper as a map to X. A convergent sequence of good parents and its bad limit form a compact set in X; its inverse image in the graph has unbounded private coordinates. The two-separator version on C_i is closed in B_i times R8, with the same distinction between private-vector infinity and true parent infinity. None of this proves nondegeneracy of kappa_i.

## 2. Common-row countermodel: all-parameter elimination

This includes h=0 and all tie strata.

For h>=0 and strict maximality, sum_i p_i=0 implies p_i>0. Put

    delta=min(p_i, p_i-p_j for j!=i)>0,
